About the creator

Graham Osborn

Educator, long-term Thailand resident, and designer of Thai Recall — a conversation companion rooted in lived practice and ELT research.

“True communication happens at the speed of life — not at the speed of a conjugation drill.”

Graham’s journey with Thai didn’t begin in a software lab. It began on the ground: living, listening, and discovering that vocabulary alone does not equal real-time speech. In markets, taxis, and cafés near temples, conversations move on while the mind is still assembling grammar.

That gap — knowing what you want to say, but not retrieving it fast enough — became the design brief for Thai Recall.

Background

Graham holds a Master of Education in Educational Technology and ELT from The University of Manchester (2003). That postgraduate foundation shapes the app’s emphasis on communicative competence, situational teaching, desirable difficulties, and spaced retrieval — theory that is meant to survive the noise of everyday Thailand.

Phrase packs are curated with care for cultural authenticity, drawing on 40 years of shared Thai heritage at home, so learners practise language people actually use — not stiff textbook Thai alone.

What he built

Thai Recall treats language as situational stock phrases you can look up, hear through a single earbud, speak immediately, save, and recall later. The guiding question for every feature:

Does this help the user stay in the conversation?
