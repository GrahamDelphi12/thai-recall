Theory that survives the street

Myth versus how memory actually works

Thai and English sit far apart on the linguistic tree. The brain that speaks them does not. Thai Recall optimises sound, situation, and retrieval — not English grammar mapped onto Thai words.

Can Western ELT ideas apply to a tonal language?

Myth: Thai is too different; Western methods won’t transfer.

Practice: Grammar systems differ; human working memory does not. Thai Recall leans on universal processes — phonological loop, noticing, chunked phrases — instead of forcing English sentence trees onto Thai.

Why a single earbud for tones?

Myth: Western speakers will always impose English pitch habits.

Practice: Reading karaoke script first often triggers those habits. Streaming native audio into one ear supports imitation while the other ear stays in the live interaction — you copy tone without leaving the room.

Phrases over isolated words?

Myth: You must master word lists and abstract rules first.

Practice: Thai thrives on formulaic sequences. Situational stock phrases — “How much is this?”, market talk, temple plans after coffee — accelerate usable fluency more than vocabulary lists alone.

Is machine translation enough?

Myth: Automation always sounds stiff or culturally off.

Practice: Live lookup is a patch for urgent utility. Core packs are curated for everyday authenticity — including review with decades of shared Thai family life — so what you practise is what locals say.

FAQ

Who is Thai Recall for?

Expats, partners, retirees, digital nomads, and frequent visitors who already understand quite a bit — and need help staying in conversation when one phrase disappears.

What if a phrase isn’t in the app?

Look it up, listen, save it. Your personal library grows from real days in Thailand, not only from a publisher’s syllabus.

Is this a subscription?

No. Android is a free evaluation now. Google Play, iOS, and Windows releases are planned.
